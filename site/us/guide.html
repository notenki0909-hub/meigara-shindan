Why this set of names (the screen)

The starting universe is the S&P Composite 1500 (S&P 500 + 400 + 600, ~1,500 names). A name is listed only if it passes both:

FilterBar
Forward dividend yield≥ 1.5% (the S&P 500 average is ~1.2%)
Consecutive years without a dividend cut≥ 5 years, computed from the yfinance dividend history (not from any external Aristocrats/Kings list)

No explicit market-cap floor — S&P 600 inclusion already implies roughly $850M+. About 500 names pass. The list is rebuilt quarterly following S&P index membership changes. Not being listed does not mean "bad" — most often the name just does not fit a dividend screen right now (e.g. a non-payer growth stock).

Data sources

  • Universe & sector (GICS): S&P 500/400/600 constituent tables (Wikipedia).
  • Prices, financials, dividends, earnings, analyst estimates: yfinance (Yahoo Finance).
  • 10-year Treasury yield (for the rate-spread metric): a bundled ~4.2% guide value, updated quarterly by hand.

All free, unauthenticated data — so errors, delays and gaps are possible. See the terms & disclaimer.

Two scores

Quality and price are kept separate.

ScoreMeansMetric groups
Selection (0–110)Quality of the dividend; is it long-term holdable Operating performance · financial strength · cash flow · dividend durability
Timing (0–110)Whether the current price looks cheap; separate from quality Dividend-yield theory · yield level & Chowder · valuation vs sector · rate spread

Read them together:

SelectionTimingReading
highhighQuality and price both look good.
highlowGood business, currently on the expensive side — a pullback candidate.
lowhighCheap but quality is in question — watch for a value trap / de-rating.
lowlowBoth quality and price look weak.

Tiers and sector grades

Tier 1 / 2 / 3 rank names within a GICS sector by selection score (top 25% / next 45% / rest). A name with "low" coverage is capped at Tier 2; a name with selection score 85+ is guaranteed at least Tier 2 so a strong sector cannot drag it to Tier 3.

Sector grade A / B / C compares GICS sectors by their median selection score (median 88+ = A, 84+ = B, below = C) — a guide to which sectors hold the better-quality dividend names. Judge individual names by the selection score and tier, not the grade.

US-specific notes

  • Total yield = dividend yield + buyback yield (buybacks / market cap). US companies return a lot of cash through repurchases, so this is scored as a first-class metric. Buybacks are a single-year figure, not a recurring commitment — don't over-rely on one year.
  • Interest coverage (EBIT / interest expense) is scored in the financial-strength group; it matters more when rates are high.
  • Rate spread (yield minus the 10-year Treasury) is scored only for rate-sensitive sectors — Utilities, Real Estate, Communication Services, Consumer Staples. US Treasury yields are high, so a spread near zero is already attractive and the thresholds are set accordingly.
  • Simple mode: banks, insurers and REITs have structurally different financials, so their operating / financial / cash-flow groups are shown for reference only and the selection score comes from dividend durability. REIT payout ratios above 100% of GAAP net income are normal (judge on FFO, which this tool does not have).
  • Company descriptions are the English text from Yahoo Finance.
